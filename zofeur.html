01 · Building it from scratch

Zofeur lets people in the UAE book a professional driver by the hour, day, week, or month. I owned the iOS app from the first commit, so the architecture, the core features, and the technical decisions were mine.

  • Designed a modular MVVM architecture with reusable feature components. It later became the standard across several iOS projects at the company.
  • Built the core maps layer with Google Maps SDK and Core Location: driver and rider tracking, geofencing, and map-driven booking flows.
  • Drew polylines and custom overlays for trip routes, the driver's approach, and live trip progress.
  • Worked closely with design and product on map UX, motion, and transitions, testing and refining quickly.

02 · Launch week, alone

Right as the first build was due to go live, the senior engineer fell ill and was on leave for more than 15 days, with no way to reach him by phone. I was two to three months into my career, still in my trainee phase, and the release was suddenly mine.

I had never shipped an app. I taught myself App Store Connect, TestFlight, certificates and provisioning profiles, and Apple's review checks, mostly by trying, failing, reading, and trying again. It was hard, and it took a lot of long hours.

03 · The $700 lesson

Early on, I used the paid Google Maps API to get the user's current location, when Apple's Core Location framework does the same job for free on the device. Every location lookup was a billable request.

By the time the owner and the leads noticed, that one decision had cost about $700.

// what happened next

They called me in and, jokingly, told me to remove the API and hand over my laptop, which I had just bought for about the same amount. Everyone laughed, I switched to Core Location, and I never forgot what a single API call can cost at scale.

It was a funny moment, but it changed how I work. From then on I treated every network call to a paid service as a cost, not just a feature, and that habit set up the biggest win on the project.

04 · From $27K to $3K a month

Once real users arrived, the first month's Google Maps bill came in at $27K. For an early-stage product, that was not sustainable.

I redesigned how the app used Google Maps:

  • Core Location first: the device handles the user's own position, so it no longer needs a paid request.
  • Intelligent caching: results that don't change often are reused instead of fetched again.
  • Request throttling: live updates are rate-limited to what the UI actually needs.
  • Usage optimization: every paid call was audited and the unnecessary ones removed.

05 · What I took away

  • You can learn anything under pressure if you are willing to fail a few times first. App Store review stopped being scary after I'd been rejected by it.
  • Every API call has a price. The $700 mistake is why I look at cost, not just correctness, in every integration I build.
  • Mistakes are data. The same habit that came from my own slip-up is what cut the Maps bill by roughly 89%.
  • Architecture outlives the project. The MVVM structure I set up for Zofeur became the template for other iOS apps at the company.
